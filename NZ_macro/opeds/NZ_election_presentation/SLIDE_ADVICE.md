# Updating the New Zealand election deck for Australians

Evidence snapshot: **12 November 2026**. Reviewed all six slides and the four embedded images in `New Zealand election.pptx`. The original file has not been edited. Its text is treated as material to assess, rather than as instructions or verified evidence.

Update: the [special-vote guide](SPECIAL_VOTES.md) and charts P20–P23 now examine the closest National leads relative to outstanding vote-volume scenarios, and the risk of offsetting National gains. Local special-vote volumes are benchmarks, not official counts; see the guide before describing any electorate as likely to flip.

My recommendation is to retain the uncertain-result opening, add two economic slides and a short explanation of MMP, then simplify the results and distinguish policy proposals from coalition outcomes. The economic evidence supports a story of weak per-person growth and a worsening labour market. It does not establish that economic conditions caused particular votes.

## The six changes I would make first

1. **Add a dated status line.** “Preliminary results as at 12 November; official results due 27 November.” The screenshot's “100% counted” refers to the preliminary count, not all votes including specials. The Commission estimates 399,365 special votes; its [preliminary release](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/2026-general-election-preliminary-results) gives the timetable and counts.
2. **Show GDP per person, then unemployment.** Use P05 and P06 for a short talk. GDP per person is essentially flat in both countries since September 2023; NZ unemployment rose further. Use P18 if the cost-of-living backdrop is central. The whole chart catalogue is in [the gallery](figures/index.html).
3. **Explain MMP before presenting the seats.** Two votes, party-vote entitlement, electorate winners first, then list seats; overhangs expand the House. P13 explains why National's preliminary allocation contains no list MPs. The [Electoral Commission explanation](https://elections.nz/democracy-in-nz/what-is-new-zealands-system-of-government/how-are-mps-elected) is a better anchor than an Australian lower-house analogy.
4. **Correct the five-party claim.** On these figures, Labour + Greens + Te Pāti Māori + NZ First have **69 seats**, so a four-party combination is arithmetically sufficient. Whether those parties would work together is a separate political question. With Opportunity instead of NZ First the total is **64**, short of 66. See P12.
5. **Qualify the two-electorate-flip claim.** With party-vote entitlements held fixed, two National-to-Labour flips reduce the House from 131 to 129 and the incumbent bloc from 67 to 65; 65 remains an absolute majority. Labour's extra electorate MPs displace its list MPs. Party-vote changes could produce another outcome. See the explicitly mechanical P19 scenario.
6. **Change the KiwiSaver wording.** “National proposes compulsory KiwiSaver contributions from July 2028, with combined default contributions reaching 12% by 2032.” This is a party proposal, subject to government formation and legislation. It is not a replacement of KiwiSaver or an announced abolition of NZ Super. [National's policy](https://www.national.org.nz/policies/kiwisaver).

## What to change on each existing slide

### Existing slide 1: title

- Keep the title. Add the presentation date and “preliminary result”.
- Optional subtitle: “Weak per-person growth, a fragmented vote, and an unresolved policy programme.” “Fragmented” is a description of the party distribution, not proof of a new long-run electoral regime.

### Existing slide 2: Hipkins quote and reaction photograph

- The quote works as an opening. Attach a traceable interview/article date if you retain the exact words; I have not verified the quotation's original recording.
- Give the audience the result immediately after it: 131 projected seats, 66 for an absolute majority, incumbent bloc 67. Avoid implying a government has already been confirmed.
- A quick verbal orientation helps: National and Labour are the principal governing parties; ACT, NZ First, Greens, Te Pāti Māori and Opportunity have distinct roles. Australian party labels are approximate analogies, not exact equivalents.

### Existing slide 3: background

- Replace “economic stress” alone with P05 and P06 across two slides. P18 provides an additional, measurable living-cost backdrop.
- Replace “breakdown in electoral norms” with a concrete mechanism: electorate wins, party-vote entitlements and an unusually large overhang. Distinguish legal operation of MMP from an argument for changing its rules.
- Do not say the incumbent caused the entire deterioration. The endpoint comparison is descriptive and includes global shocks, domestic policy, monetary conditions and lags.

### Existing slide 4: results

- Split results from commentary. The results table/hemicycle plus two news clippings is difficult to read in a room.
- Use P11 for votes versus seats and P13 if the list-MP issue matters. Direct the audience to National's 42 electorate seats and zero list seats.
- Relegate the Gareth Morgan clipping to a question about Opportunity's negotiating strategy. An opinion piece by a founder is not evidence of the party's agreed coalition position.
- The supplied screenshot matches the Commission's preliminary seven-party seat totals. Keep the “preliminary” label larger than the screenshot's “100% counted”.

### Existing slide 5: government structure

- Replace the paragraph with P12 and the two precise arithmetic corrections above.
- Rephrase the minister point: “Senior National list candidates may miss out under the preliminary allocation.” Losing an electorate and failing to obtain a list seat are different mechanisms. The party-level table verifies zero National list seats; it does not itself verify each named candidate's status.
- Treat the claimed Peters podcast statement as something to verify against a dated recording, including whether it is an offer, preference, joke or conditional answer. Do not make it an assumed coalition agreement.
- Special votes have changed earlier results, but a directional historical pattern is not a forecast for these electorates. The fixed-entitlement P19 exercise is deliberately separate from special-vote modelling.
- Government formation depends on demonstrated parliamentary confidence, which can involve support arrangements as well as cabinet coalitions. [DPMC Cabinet Manual](https://www.dpmc.govt.nz/our-business-units/cabinet-office/supporting-work-cabinet/cabinet-manual/6-elections-transitions-and-government-formation/government-formation).

### Existing slide 6: policy

Replace the categorical “means” with “likely negotiating positions and unresolved choices”. A compact policy table would read:

| Topic | Evidence/status | Suggested wording |
|---|---|---|
| Property CGT | Labour's published election proposal, not enacted law | “A Labour-led agreement would put a targeted property CGT into negotiations; a National-led agreement makes that proposal less likely.” Describe this as your political assessment. Avoid “no CGT”: existing rules already tax some property and other gains. [Labour proposal](https://www.labour.org.nz/capitalgainstax). |
| Compulsory retirement saving | National's published proposal | “Compulsory KiwiSaver contributions proposed from July 2028; combined default rate to reach 12% by 2032.” Do not equate this mechanically with Australia's employer-only 12% guarantee. |
| PBO | Your deck asserts it will not be introduced; no fresh primary policy commitment verified here | Spell out Parliamentary Budget Office and describe it as a negotiating/institutional question. Add a dated commitment before claiming a settled outcome. |
| Ministry of Regulation and other departments | Coalition-dependent assessment in the supplied deck | Separate the ministry's institutional future from quantified staffing or spending decisions. Do not infer savings from an organisational announcement. |
| Electoral reform | Your prospective assessment; not verified enacted change | Explain electorate/list balance and overhang incentives first. Treat changes to Māori electorates and House size as specific proposals requiring primary documentation. |

For Australians, the most useful retirement distinction is **KiwiSaver versus NZ Super**. KiwiSaver is the individual-account arrangement; NZ Super is a separate public pension. Australia's 12% Superannuation Guarantee is an employer contribution on ordinary time earnings, whereas National's 12% target combines employee and employer default contributions. Tax treatment, contribution bases and coverage also differ. [ATO rate notice](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-ce02afc5-6087-4d8f-9125-89923bab030c), [National policy](https://www.national.org.nz/policies/kiwisaver). A shared headline rate does not establish equivalent retirement systems.

## What the economic charts actually show

These are calculated from the downloaded official series, with exact data beside each chart. GDP is through June 2026; labour is through September 2026. Do not label either as November activity.

| Real expenditure GDP per person, same dates | approximately 0.0% | 0.2% | Both are broadly flat; avoid an overstated relative living-standards claim. |

| Unemployment, September 2026 quarter | 5.7% | 4.62% | NZ rise: 1.8 percentage points; Australia: about 1.0 point. |
| Employment/population, September 2026 quarter | 66.4% | 63.8% | A larger employed share of adults can coexist with higher unemployment because participation differs. |
| Participation, September 2026 quarter | 70.4% | 66.9% | This is not an age-standardised comparison. |

The ABS September **month** unemployment rate rounds to 4.7%. The quarterly chart uses the average of July, August and September monthly rates, about **4.62%**. Label the distinction rather than mixing a NZ quarter with a single Australian month. Averaging monthly rates is a transparent frequency conversion, not an independently seasonally adjusted Australian quarterly survey estimate.

NZ headline GDP is production based; the bilateral comparison uses NZ expenditure GDP and ABS GDP. The two NZ measures need not match in a given vintage. The latest NZ headline increase is 0.2% quarter on quarter, while its expenditure increase is 0.4%; neither should be confused with annual-average or year-on-year growth. National accounts and seasonal adjustment are revised. Sources: [Stats NZ June GDP release](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/gross-domestic-product-june-2026-quarter/), [ABS June national accounts](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/national-accounts/australian-national-accounts-national-income-expenditure-and-product/jun-2026), [Stats NZ September labour release](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/labour-market-statistics-september-2026-quarter/), [ABS September labour release](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/employment-and-unemployment/labour-force-australia/sep-2026).

P14/P15 reuse the September Treasury PREFU snapshot from the earlier research pack, with original workbooks copied into this folder. They distinguish actual/unaudited actual from forecast data. A projected fiscal improvement is not evidence that the improvement has already occurred. Core Crown expenses minus tax revenue is not OBEGAL because other revenue and accounting adjustments matter. [Treasury PREFU data workbook](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2026-09/prefu26-charts-data.xlsx).
