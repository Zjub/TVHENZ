Unemployment The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was 5.7 percent in the September 2026 quarter, compared with 5.6 percent in the June 2026 quarter. For men, the unemployment rate was unchanged at 5.6 percent. For women, the unemployment rate was 5.7 percent, compared with 5.5 percent last quarter.

The seasonally adjusted number of unemployed people was 174,000, compared with 171,000 in the June 2026 quarter. 1,000 fewer men were unemployed. 3,000 more women were unemployed. The following data is not seasonally adjusted. In the September 2026 quarter, the unemployment rate for ethnic groups was: 11.5 percent for Pacific peoples 10.9 percent for Māori 5.0 percent for Asian 4.5 percent for European. Of the 169,100 people who were unemployed in the September 2026 quarter (not seasonally adjusted), 21.5 percent had been unemployed for more than a year. Being unemployed for over a year is one measure of long-term unemployment.

Underutilisation The seasonally adjusted underutilisation rate was unchanged at 13.8 percent in the September 2026 quarter. For men, the underutilisation rate was unchanged at 12.4 percent. For women, the underutilisation rate was 15.3 percent, compared with 15.2 percent last quarter.

Underutilisation is a broad measure of spare capacity in New Zealand’s labour market and is just as important as the unemployment rate, as it gives us a more detailed picture of the workforce. In the September 2026 quarter, the underutilisation rate for ethnic groups was: 22.3 percent for Māori 21.2 percent for Pacific peoples 12.7 percent for Asian 12.0 percent for European. Underutilisation in the North Island increased to 14.0 percent in the September 2026 quarter, up 1.4 percentage points from a year earlier. In the South Island, the underutilisation rate was 11.7 percent. A regional breakdown of underutilisation can be found in the tables. Employment The seasonally adjusted employment rate was 66.4 percent, compared with 66.7 percent last quarter. For men, the employment rate was 70.1 percent, compared with 70.4 percent last quarter. For women, the employment rate was 62.8 percent, compared with 63.1 percent last quarter.

In the September 2026 quarter, 2,891,000 people were employed, compared with 2,896,000 in the June 2026 quarter and 2,872,000 in the September 2025 quarter. The following data is not seasonally adjusted. Annually, there was an increase in the number of paid employees working in casual or fixed term roles. Among 2,340,000 paid employees in the September 2026 quarter, there were: 2,136,700 permanent employees 116,900 casual employees (up 19,100 annually) 50,900 fixed-term employees (up 8,100 annually) 35,400 seasonal, temporary agency, or other employee types not elsewhere included. Information about employment permanency is only collected for paid employees, not for self-employed people or unpaid family workers. For more frequent, administrative-based indicators of levels and changes in filled jobs, see our monthly Employment indicators . Hours worked The seasonally adjusted number of hours usually and actually worked decreased in the September 2026 quarter. Usual hours decreased by 0.49 million (down 0.5 percent) to 106.5 million hours. Actual hours decreased by 1.56 million (down 1.6 percent) to 94.7 million hours.

Hours usually worked represent an average work week across all employed people, while hours actually worked cover the week prior to being surveyed, including overtime and time spent away from work. The decrease in actual and usual hours worked coincides with 15,000 more people employed part-time and 17,000 fewer people employed full-time in the September 2026 quarter (seasonally adjusted). 11,000 more men were employed part-time. 3,000 more women were employed part-time. 16,000 fewer men were employed full-time. 2,000 fewer women were employed full-time. Annually, the number of people working fewer hours than usual in their main job increased by 78,100 (21.7 percent) to 438,100 (not seasonally adjusted). This came predominantly from an increase in the number of people who said they worked fewer hours than usual for holiday reasons (up 59,200 to 146,900). Holiday reasons can relate to vacations, school holidays, public holidays, or anything else that might be considered by the respondent as ‘holiday reasons’. In 2026, the Matariki public holiday fell within the September quarter, while in 2024 and 2025, it fell within June quarters. Labour force participation The seasonally adjusted labour force participation rate was 70.4 percent, compared with 70.6 percent in the June 2026 quarter. For men, the labour force participation rate was 74.3 percent, compared with 74.6 percent last quarter. For women, the labour force participation rate was 66.6 percent, compared with 66.7 percent last quarter.

Youth not in employment, education, or training (NEET) In the September 2026 quarter, the seasonally adjusted proportion of people aged 15 to 24 years who were not in employment, education, or training (NEET) was 14.1 percent, compared with 13.8 percent last quarter.

Wages The labour cost index (LCI) all salary and wage rates (including overtime) increased 2.1 percent in the year to the September 2026 quarter, while the unadjusted LCI increased 2.9 percent. Private sector wages increased by 2.1 percent over the year. Public sector wages increased by 1.9 percent over the year. The LCI is often compared with the consumers price index (CPI) to see how wage inflation compares with consumer inflation (that is, the change in prices of goods and services bought by households). Annual CPI inflation was 3.7 percent in the year to the September 2026 quarter.

In the year to the September 2026 quarter, wages as measured by the Quarterly Employment Survey (QES) also grew. Average ordinary time hourly earnings increased to $44.86 (up 2.9 percent). Private sector ordinary time hourly earnings increased by 2.6 percent, to $42.58. Public sector ordinary time hourly earnings increased by 3.6 percent, to $53.14. Average weekly earnings (including overtime) for full-time equivalent employees (FTEs) in the QES also increased on an annual basis, up 3.3 percent to $1,743. Private sector average weekly earnings increased by 2.9 percent, to $1,649. Public sector average weekly earnings increased by 4.3 percent, to $2,095.
