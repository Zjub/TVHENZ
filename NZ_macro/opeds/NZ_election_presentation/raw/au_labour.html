Key statistics

In seasonally adjusted terms, in September 2026:

  • the employment-to-population ratio decreased 0.3ppt to 63.6%.
  • the unemployment rate increased 0.1ppt to 4.7%.

In trend terms, in September 2026:

  • the employment-to-population ratio remained at 63.8%.
  • the unemployment rate remained at 4.6%.

Survey impacts and changes

Labour Force Modernisation

As part of the Labour Force modernisation program, the ABS made improvements to select questions in the Labour Force questionnaire. As a result, there will be a lower degree of comparability for the period before, during, and after the transition period for certain data items. This includes:

  • Temporary increased variability in the Labour force status and Gross changes (LMS1) pivot table, as a result of changes to the number of incoming and outgoing rotation groups between May and August 2026.
  • Changes to short-term and medium-term unemployed persons by duration of job search in Table 025, and both the UDJ1 and UDJ2 pivots tables, a result of short term unemployment now being defined as one month instead of four weeks.
  • New categories in the collection of reasons for fewer than usual hours worked in both pivots HRS3 and HRS4.

Classification changes to occupation related data

The ABS updated the occupation standards classification in 2024 for Australian statistics. Occupation data from this release onwards is categorised using the Occupation Standard Classification for Australia, version 1.0 (OSCA). Estimates from August 2006, originally collected using the previous standard (ANZSCO, 2013 v1.3), have been re-coded using the OSCA classification to allow a cohesive time series to be released.

Occupation data is available in the Data downloads section of this release in Tables 023, 024, X30, IAO3, IAO4 and IAO5.

Weighting adjustments in Victoria

For the September 2026 data release, the ABS adjusted the influence of around 1,400 respondents (approximately 2.8% of the September sample) in one rotation group in Victoria. This was done by using adjusted weights in line with our well-established methods to ensure that the Labour Force data is representative of the population.

The ABS will continue to monitor this rotation group as they continue to progress through their time in our survey.

Rotation group analysis

The Labour Force Survey is subject to sample variability on a month-to-month basis. For example, the August outgoing rotation group had a lower unemployment rate than that of the incoming rotation group in September, which was closer to the overall result.

As in August, there may be a degree of residual influence on the September 2026 data due to changes in Supplementary Survey collection methodology.

Quality assurance checks undertaken by the ABS indicate the September Labour Force statistics are fit-for-use.

For further information, refer to Supplementary Survey methodology changes in the Labour Force Survey.

Data downloads

The release of the September 2026 Labour Force, Australia marks the return to the full suite of Labour Force data products.

From the September 2026 reference period onwards, our monthly publications include:

  1. Labour Force, Australia headline spreadsheets and pivot tables, released on the third Thursday of each month.
  2. Longitudinal Labour Force, Australia microdata released in ABS DataLab six days after the release of the headline estimates.
  3. A new Regional Labour Force publication released one week after the headline estimates.
  4. TableBuilder: Labour Force Survey released eight days after the headline estimates.
  5. Labour Force PLIDA Modular Product released approximately 2 weeks after the headline estimates.

There may be some shifts in detailed labour force timeseries data following improvements to select questions and rotation group patterns as part of Labour Force Modernisation. For further information, see Survey impacts and changes.
