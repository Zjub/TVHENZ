Taxation of income
Understanding taxation of capital gains requires thinking about income tax.
Tax changes in the capacity to consume irrespective of how we do so.
Tax capital like wages? Intergenerational equity? Equity-efficiency trade-off.
What is income?
Capital income as a return to savings.
Inflation reduces the value of nominal savings – not income.
A normal rate of return compensates for deferring consumption – necessary to maintain present value of savings, so not income.
To tax all true income the same way we can either tax consumption, or tax labour income + capital income above a normal rate of return.
Hard to create the redistribution we may want with consumption taxes (progressivity). Hence an income tax is needed.

What are capital gains and negative gearing
A capital gain is an additional form of capital income.
Like other capital income, some of the dollar amount is not “income”.
Is taxed on realisation – this gives capital gain a deferral benefit relative to other capital income currently.
Due to progressive taxes, capital gains are taxed at a higher rate than if they were taxed each year.

What are capital gains and negative gearing
Negative gearing is when annual revenue for an asset in a year is less than the costs, due to borrowing expenses.
Only allow expenses related to taxed income. Allowing deductibility of inflation component of interest inconsistent with discount/indexation.
Quarantining losses both punishes those with genuine losses (as all expenses are deferred) and over-credits those making positive annual rental income.

Trade-offs
Capital mobility and investment
Changing residence shifts taxation of “passive” housing capital gains.
Mobile ideas and finance can move – incidence passes through to consumers and business.
Domestic value-add is less mobile.
International evidence suggests mobility from CGT is limited.
Also suggests the general tax rate mobility is lower for sources that generate high returns (location specific rents).
