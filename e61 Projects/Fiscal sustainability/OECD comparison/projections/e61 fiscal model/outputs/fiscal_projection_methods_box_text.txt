Scope and starting point. The e61 fiscal model projects consolidated Australian general-government spending, revenue and debt from 2029-30 to 2065-66. Transfers between governments are consolidated. The 2029-30 fiscal totals and debt stocks are anchored to the PBO National Fiscal Outlook. ABS Government Finance Statistics (GFS) provide 2024-25 spending and revenue composition; programme allocations are projected to 2030 and reconciled to the PBO totals. These allocations are e61 estimates, not PBO programme forecasts. [1, 2]
Spending method. Each spending category combines demographic demand, coverage, input costs, service intensity and policy settings. Annual IGR age/sex population projections drive age composition; changing total population growth scales all age/sex cells equally. Fixed IGR age-cost profiles proxy health and aged-care demand; education uses age-band populations with fixed participation. AIHW funding data allocate health sub-functions within the ABS total, so these are not IGR programme categories. NDIA participation and age-specific budget/utilisation data inform NDIS demand, with an assumed retention rule for older cohorts. [3-5]
Costs and service standards. Real wages follow economy-wide productivity. Health and education resource-cost baskets combine labour and non-labour inputs; observed cost shares are used where available, with proxies elsewhere. Non-labour inputs follow domestic prices; capital costs are not separately projected. Service efficiency defaults to 25% of economy-wide productivity growth in applicable baskets, rather than in transfers, medicines or the VET funding-index rule. Additional income-related service intensity has elasticity 0.8 for health and 0.5 for education with respect to real GDP per person. Health uses an internationally informed scenario coefficient; education 0.5 is an unverified assumption. Both act beyond explicit input costs. [6, 7]
Reference assumptions. Annual labour productivity grows 1.2%, domestic prices 2.5% and population 1.2%; average hours and the terms of trade are constant. Nominal GDP is implied by output, prices and terms of trade. IGR aggregate participation paths shape employment. Remaining health spending pressure defaults to zero; education also defaults to zero additional annual growth. Efficiency and additional growth are unestimated assumptions; review overlap with income-related intensity. [3]
Revenue and debt. Personal income tax applies tax schedules to ATO income-percentile cells, with wage-indexed thresholds by default. Other receipts follow structural tax-base proxies; company tax is a calibrated GDP share linked to terms of trade. Age Pension receipt reflects public-data cohort wealth and means tests. The reference assumes 4.0% real superannuation returns and a nominal borrowing rate converging to 4.4% by 2040. Interest is paid on gross debt; financial assets earn interest. Deficits and net investment feed debt accumulation; no automatic tax increase or spending cut stabilises debt. [1, 2, 8]
Interpretation. These are conditional scenarios, not validated forecasts. Published data supply levels and profiles; e61 calibration, proxies and unverified assumptions supply the remaining structure. Reported scenarios must state departures from these reference settings. Real per-person spending uses 2030 prices and total residents; decomposition bars allocate interactions across drivers using Shapley accounting and do not identify causal effects.
